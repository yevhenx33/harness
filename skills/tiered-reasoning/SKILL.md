# Tiered Reasoning

Use only when the user explicitly assigns T0, T1, T2, or T3 for the current
problem. Never infer a tier or carry it into another problem. Preserve the user's
model and effort choice; a tier does not change authority, budget, or acceptance.

Use a single agent. Do not create worker lanes, additional worktrees for operators,
fresh-agent verifiers, or cross-chat orchestration. If the runtime cannot apply a
requested effort setting, report the mismatch; do not claim it was applied or
spawn another executor as a workaround. Hold work that depends on the exact setting.

| Tier | Requested effort | Single-agent method |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | low | Direct mechanism and focused oracle. |
| T1 | medium | Primary mechanism; challenge material assumptions. |
| T2 | high | Compare credible structural alternatives and falsifiers. |
| T3 | xhigh | Examine distinct causal mechanisms, then challenge the recommendation against raw evidence. |

Freeze the concrete contract, baseline, oracle, and cumulative cost. Compare
alternatives only while material uncertainty remains; do not require a candidate
quota. Preserve causal differences rather than cosmetic variants. For noisy
comparisons control workload, environment, run order, warmup, and aggregation.

Reject violations of outcome, authority, invariants, or budget before comparing
performance. Never average incompatible assumptions or combine every proposed
change. A hybrid needs its own parity and verification. Sequential challenges are
not independent model review; rely on direct calculations, checks, and consumer
oracles. Apply root completion passes and report limitations.
